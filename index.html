Facility network & access overlays

?

Distance to nearest facility — minimum kilometers from any point in Guinea to a facility offering this test.

Population × distance — population-weighted access burden; high values flag dense areas with poor access.

Population outside 20 km walkshed — populations beyond a 20 km walking radius from a facility offering this test.

Lab tests often can’t be processed where they’re collected — samples travel up the network from center → prefecture → region → national. These overlays measure how far that journey is for each rapid test, three ways: distance to the nearest capable facility, population × distance (where access burden concentrates), or population beyond a 20 km walkshed.

Why measure? Cold-chain storage has a maximum viable transport time, and rainy-season road washouts can break the network. Route estimates come from Google Maps and may not match ground conditions.

About

An interactive view over a 2016 LAB-NET survey of ~106 medical facilities in Guinea — sample-transport routes, lab capabilities, and three measures of geographic access by rapid test type.

Facility locations are real surveyed sites. Survey response values shown in this public version have been randomized to protect respondent privacy.

How to use

  • Click any chart bar or map cluster to filter — all other views update.
  • Use the Capabilities chips to narrow by amenity or rapid test.
  • The map overlay shows access burden by test type.
  • Mouseover a facility to see possible sample-transport routes.

Reading the map

Markers cluster by administrative level — national (orange), regional (purple), prefecture (green), and center-level (blue).

Heatmap methodology

The three inaccessibility overlays were developed for the ASTMH 2018 poster Spatial Analysis of Health Facility Access in Guinea:

    Maciej Boni, Kory Blose, Stephen Fast, Robbie Fraleigh

    Siaka Ouattara, Moussa Condé, Marcel Yaradouno, Isabelle Amblard
  • Guinea Ministry of Health & Public Hygiene
    Mamadou S. Bah

LAB-NET partners: French Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Expertise France, Institut Pasteur. Survey collection by Fondation Mérieux. Route estimates from Google Maps.
